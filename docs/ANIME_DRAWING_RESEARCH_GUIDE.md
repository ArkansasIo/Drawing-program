# Anime Studio Drawing & Visual Development Research Guide\n\n## Purpose\nAnime Studio should generate original characters, animals, humans, vehicles, clothing, props, environments, effects, and animation-ready parts using a modular 2D asset pipeline that can also place those assets into a 3D/2.5D stage.\n\nThe target is not a clone of any particular franchise. The system uses broad visual principles from Japanese animation, Western feature animation, comics, illustration, and modern digital production.\n\n## 1. Character construction\n- Configurable body presets: child, chibi, stylized adolescent, heroic adult, slender, athletic, heavy/powerful, creature-humanoid.\n- Skeleton landmarks: skull, jaw, neck, clavicle, rib cage, pelvis, shoulders, elbows, wrists, hands, hips, knees, ankles and feet.\n- Head construction: cranium, jaw/chin, centerline, eye line, brow line, nose/mouth landmarks, ears and hair mass.\n- Views: front, profile, three-quarter, high-angle and low-angle.\n\n## 2. Anime facial design\nUse independent reusable components:\n- eye shapes, irises, pupils, eyebrows, eyelids, eyelashes\n- noses, mouths, ears, blush and face shadows\n- expressions: neutral, happy, excited, angry, sad, worried, surprised, embarrassed, determined, tired and frightened.\n\n## 3. Hair generator\nTreat hair as grouped masses rather than individual strands. Generate bangs, side locks, rear masses, ponytails, braids, buns, short/long hair, spiky, layered, curly/wavy and fantasy styles.\nParameters: length, volume, direction, strand grouping, taper, highlights, shadow depth and wind direction.\n\n## 4. Clothing generator\nUpper body: shirts, tunics, jackets, coats, robes, hooded garments, uniforms, armor, breastplates, shoulder guards, capes and scarves.\nLower body: trousers, shorts, skirts, leggings, greaves, belts and utility pouches.\nHands/feet: gloves, gauntlets, wrist guards, boots, sandals and shoes.\nEvery garment should support attachment points, front/back layers, seams, fold guides, thickness, material, color, pattern, wear, dirt, damage, accessories and deformation hints.\n\n## 5. Fabric drawing\nUse selective folds: compression, tension, hanging, radial and overlapping folds. Fold density depends on pose, stiffness, fit, gravity, movement, wind and contact points.\n\n## 6. Color and cel shading\nUse managed palette families for skin, hair, eyes, clothing, metal, leather, wood, stone, vegetation, magic, sky, shadows, highlights and atmosphere.\nEach material can expose base color, shadow, deep shadow, highlight, rim light and emissive/accent color.\nPreset families: classic fantasy anime, dark fantasy, supernatural action, warm adventure, night, sunset, moonlit blue, neon fantasy, historical/fantasy and monochrome manga.\nCel lighting should support base, shadow, deep shadow, highlight and rim-light bands with adjustable thresholds, color temperature and outline strength.\n\n## 7. Animals, creatures and anatomy\nUse real zoological anatomy as reference while keeping generated designs original.\nCreature categories: mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, insects, arachnids, marine animals, dinosaurs/extinct animals and fantasy hybrids.\nModular parts: skull/head, torso, spine, limbs, paws/hooves/claws, tail, ears/horns, wings, beak/muzzle, eyes, fur/feathers/scales and markings.\nProvide optional educational overlays for skeleton, major muscles, joints, hands, feet, facial muscles and body planes.\n\n## 8. Vehicles and machines\nUse real engineering references for believable silhouettes while keeping fictional designs original.\nCategories: cars, trucks, motorcycles, trains, aircraft, helicopters, ships, boats, spacecraft, mecha, construction and emergency vehicles.\nModular parts: chassis, wheels/tracks, suspension, cockpit, doors, windows, engines, exhaust, lights, cargo, decals and damage states.\nSupport front, side, rear, top and three-quarter views.\n\n## 9. Props and environments\nGenerate weapons, tools, furniture, books, food, instruments, signs, containers, lamps, architecture, vegetation, rocks, buildings, interiors and fantasy structures.\nSupport perspective guides and consistent vanishing points.\n\n## 10. Backgrounds and 2.5D\nLayer backgrounds into sky, distant atmosphere, far background, middle ground, foreground and FX overlays. These layers become depth cards or planes for parallax.\n\n## 11. Animation principles\nSupport squash/stretch, anticipation, staging, straight-ahead and pose-to-pose animation, follow-through, overlapping action, slow-in/slow-out, arcs, secondary action, timing, spacing, exaggeration, solid drawing and appeal.\nAnime production can combine limited animation with detailed key poses, camera movement, effects, holds, smears, speed lines and impact frames.\n\n## 12. Western feature-animation principles\nUse broad production principles such as clear silhouette, readable pose, character appeal, strong staging, acting through pose/expression, visual storytelling, exaggeration, anticipation, follow-through, composition, thumbnails, model sheets, expression sheets, turnarounds, color scripts, storyboards and animatics.\nThese are general production principles, not copied proprietary artwork or characters.\n\n## 13. Generated asset architecture\nEvery asset should have: asset ID, category, tags, source/reference metadata, palette, style preset, proportions, view, layer list, editable components, licensing/provenance and revision number.\nExample character components: head, hair, eyes, eyebrows, mouth, neck, torso, arms, hands, legs, feet, clothing, accessories, shadow, highlight and FX.\n\n## 14. 2D-to-3D staging\n2D artwork remains the source. The stage adds X/Y/Z position, rotation, scale, depth, camera projection, parallax, lighting, fog, atmospheric depth, camera shake and bookmarks.\nThis enables 2.5D anime scenes without requiring every character to become a fully modeled 3D character.\n\n## 15. Generator families\nHuman, face, hairstyle, clothing, pose, expression, hand/foot, animal, creature, vehicle, prop, environment, background, FX, palette, lighting and camera/composition generators.\n\n## 16. Core design rule\nGenerated assets must remain editable components rather than only flattened pictures. A character should allow hair, clothing, expression, pose, palette, lighting and camera to change independently.
